\section{Results}

We evaluated the representation in two simulation scenarios and one archived empirical example. These analyses were
designed as demonstrations of the geometry of tree signatures. They were not replicated sufficiently to support formal
comparisons between algorithms, and the empirical example is reported as exploratory pending reconstruction from a
versioned data release.

\subsection{SPR-chain simulation with missing taxa}

Starting from a random tree on 16 leaves, we generated a chain in which consecutive trees differed by an SPR move. Four
groups of ten gene trees were sampled from separated portions of the chain. Eight leaves were removed from each sampled
tree; trees in the same group shared the same removed leaves. Reference trees retained all leaves. We compared an
informed reference collection drawn from portions of the SPR chain with a collection of random reference trees.

Figure~\ref{figure002} shows metric multidimensional-scaling (MDS) projections of the resulting signatures. The four
generating groups are visually separated with the informed landmarks. Separation is also apparent with random
landmarks, although the geometry changes. Because taxon-removal patterns were shared within groups, this demonstration
does not distinguish phylogenetic signal from signal introduced by missingness. No accuracy statistic or uncertainty
interval was recorded for the archived run.

The trees in this scenario are single-copy and can therefore also be compared by conventional pairwise distances. An
archived exploratory comparison used normalised RF and approximate SPR pairwise distances and produced qualitatively
similar MDS plots; those unrecorded plots are not treated as evidence here.

\subsection{Genome-level simulation}

The second scenario used SimPhy \citep{Mallo2015-ag} to generate gene trees within locus trees evolving inside species
trees. The archived notebook generated four species trees with 20 species and 50 gene families per species tree, sampled
one or two individuals per species, and removed up to approximately half of the gene-tree leaves. Reference trees were
generated by applying SPR perturbations to the four species trees; the true species trees themselves were excluded from
the signature basis.

The archived parameterisation included duplication, loss, transfer, and incomplete lineage sorting. Figure
\ref{figure003} shows the family-size and taxon-overlap distributions and four two-dimensional projections from one run.
MDS and Isomap show visible separation among the four generating species trees. The t-SNE projection overlaps strongly,
and the spectral embedding arranges the groups along narrow branches. Thus, the demonstration is sensitive to the
projection method and should not be interpreted as a general clustering benchmark.

\subsection{Archived fungal example}

The archived fungal analysis used root hierarchical orthologous groups (root HOGs) from an approximately 2018 OMA
snapshot. At a specified taxonomic level, a HOG represents orthologs and paralogs descending from a common ancestral gene
\citep{Altenhoff2024OMA}. The original analysis records do not identify a frozen OMA release, HOG manifest, taxon list,
or complete gene-tree inference pipeline. Approximate counts are retained here only to document the historical proof of
concept: about 11,000 gene families across 49 species, with approximately 50 high-coverage root HOGs and 120 additional
large families used to help infer reference trees.

Reference candidates were inferred with \textit{guenomu} \citep{Martins2016}, augmented by topology perturbations, and
contrasted with randomised references. The archived notes describe approximately 250 informed and 250 random reference
trees. The downstream analysis used the empirical MinMax block and omitted the empirical-rank block because the latter
did not appear useful in preliminary projections. No predefined criterion for that decision was recorded.

Sparse principal-component analysis (PCA) selected different mixtures of dissimilarities when using informed and random
reference trees (Figures~\ref{figure005} and \ref{figure006}). For the informed references, individual components were
often dominated by one or two dissimilarity types. The random-reference components were less readily attributable to a
single type. This is a descriptive loading analysis; it does not establish that a selected dissimilarity corresponds to
a unique evolutionary process.

(Figure~\ref{figure007}). The colours in all panels are five-means labels fitted to the full feature matrix; they are not
external biological annotations. Their persistence across projections illustrates the geometry imposed by that fitted
partition, but does not validate five biological classes or show that informed references outperform random references.

\subsection{Reproducibility status}

The simulations can be reconstructed from notebooks in the software repository, but they currently contain absolute
paths, unrecorded random states, and version-dependent analysis calls. The empirical input data and analysis code are not
present. Consequently, all results in this section should be considered provisional until the workflows are made
portable, parameterised, and repeatable from archived inputs.
