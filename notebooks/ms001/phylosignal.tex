\begin{abstract}
Phylogenomic data sets contain gene trees that differ because of biological processes, sampling, and inference error.
Direct comparison is particularly difficult when gene families contain paralogs, differ in taxon coverage, or have few
species in common. We introduce a landmark representation in which each gene family tree is mapped to a fixed-length
vector of dissimilarities to a common collection of reference species trees. The dissimilarities describe complementary
aspects of gene--species tree disagreement, including duplication, loss, deep coalescence, Robinson--Foulds,
split-matching, and approximate subtree-prune-and-regraft scores. Once the reference trees are fixed, new gene trees can
be projected independently without recomputing an all-pairs distance matrix. Simulations with missing taxa and
multi-copy gene trees, together with an exploratory fungal data set, illustrate how these vectors can be visualised and
clustered. The present analyses establish a proof of concept: replicated benchmarking, explicit treatment of gene-tree
uncertainty, and biological validation of the empirical clusters remain necessary before comparative performance or
biological conclusions can be claimed.
\end{abstract}
